Reporting is triggered by a meaningful consequence, not every task or every file edit.
When an existing RETURN already states result, source revision, affected contract and
uncertainty, use it. Do not require the head to write a second report, a second journal
entry and a second summary for the same event. Promote a keeper to its existing relevant
evidence home; an already durable receipt can simply be linked.

If a proposed change exceeds authorized scope, breaks a declared interface or collides
with another writer, resolve that existing constraint with its owner before the dependent
change. A flag amendment requires majkee's gavel. This is not a new Houston checkpoint,
and not a reason to treat every move or state-path edit as an architectural decision.

## Lightest seed for a new nest

An optional `architecture-note.md` is justified only when it saves the new head real
research. Otherwise a short section in the incoming POINT/brief is enough. Four things:

1. The desired outcome and why it matters.
2. The few relevant source/raw pointers, with what remains uncertain.
3. Existing contracts and affected neighbors; distinguish locks from suggestions.
4. The observable result that would settle the question.

The cSharp owns its RUNBOOK and grows the local experiment from there. Houston does
not prescribe substeps, worker choices or the whole folder tree. It may offer competing
interpretations so the nest can investigate instead of inheriting a premature conclusion.
Raw material stays in its current durable home when possible; transfer a scoped pointer
bundle, not every transcript. Valuable raw evidence is promoted explicitly before pruning.

For this conversation, majkee's `(?)` means: give the strongest useful alternative and
creative challenge. It is not a request for automatic agreement or a new global rule.

## Houston's standing content

Keep the purpose/narrative, major component boundaries, interfaces, source and delivery
homes, and the reasons behind consequential decisions discoverable. Track the important
unknowns through their owning documents. Indicate whether a claim is intended, authored,
installed or actually exercised; a source update does not renew a host-behavior receipt.

**Coding standards:** Houston should help reconcile standards with the architecture and
point to their existing owners. Keep concrete rules beside the code or in established
project instructions and formatter/linter/test configuration. Do not maintain a second
Houston copy of those rules. A proposed cross-part convention needs its rationale and
review; Houston's preference alone does not make it a mandatory coding standard.

Other responsibilities worth retaining: notice unowned shared files, expose the cost of
adding a dependency, preserve the reason a design was rejected, and help retire obsolete
interfaces/docs. Name one writer and an order when two sessions touch the same shared file.
Architecture grows through those concrete needs, not a catalogue of every possible duty.
